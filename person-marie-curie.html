Physicist · Chemist

Marie Curie

The first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences, and a pioneer of radioactivity research.

Known for
Radioactivity research; Nobel Prizes in Physics (1903) and Chemistry (1911)
Born
7 November 1867
Died
4 July 1934
Country
Poland / France

Maria Skłodowska was born in Warsaw at a time when women could not attend university there. She studied in secret, worked as a governess to fund her sister's education, and finally moved to Paris to study physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne.

With her husband Pierre Curie she investigated the mysterious rays emitted by uranium. Their work led to the discovery of two new elements, polonium — named after her homeland — and radium. She coined the term "radioactivity."

In 1903 she shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel. In 1911 she won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry on her own, becoming the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

During the First World War she helped develop mobile X-ray units that were used to treat wounded soldiers near the front. Her long exposure to radiation, whose dangers were not then understood, is believed to have contributed to her death in 1934.

Why her story matters

Curie persisted through exclusion, poverty and grief, and put her discoveries to practical use for others. Her career still inspires scientists, especially women entering fields where they are underrepresented.
