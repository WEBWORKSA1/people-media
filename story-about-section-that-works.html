Write an About Section That Gets You Hired, Booked or Funded

Your About section is often the first thing a decision-maker reads. Most are written for the writer. Here's how to write one for the reader.

By People.Media Editorial·18 August 2026·6 min read
Career

About sections on websites, professional networks and speaker profiles share a common flaw: they read like résumés. Decision-makers skim them looking for one thing — evidence that you can solve their problem. Write for that reader.

The four-paragraph structure

  1. Who you help and how. One or two sentences. Use your one-sentence promise from our personal brand playbook.
  2. Proof. Three concrete results, clients, publications or achievements.
  3. Your story. Why you do this work — a short, specific origin.
  4. Next step. Exactly what the reader should do: book a call, read a case study, subscribe.

First person or third person?

First person feels warmer on personal websites and professional networks. Third person suits speaker bios, press kits and award submissions. Keep both versions ready.

Specific beats impressive

"Passionate, results-driven leader" tells the reader nothing. "Led a team of eight that cut customer wait times from three days to four hours" tells them everything. Replace adjectives with evidence wherever you can.

Keep it current

Review your About section every quarter. Add new features, results and projects. Remove anything older and weaker. A stale profile quietly signals a stalled career.

Checklist

  • Can a stranger tell what you do in five seconds?
  • Is there at least one number or named result?
  • Does it sound like you when read aloud?
  • Is there a clear call to action?
  • Are links and contact options working?
