The Ethics of Sharing Someone's Story Online

Consent, privacy and dignity are not legal fine print. They are the foundation of storytelling people can trust.

By People.Media Editorial·04 September 2026·7 min read
Ethics

Sharing a person's story can bring them support, recognition and connection. It can also expose them to judgement, harassment or harm they did not anticipate. Responsible storytellers weigh both. These principles guide every story People.Media publishes, and they are a useful checklist for anyone who shares stories online.

1. Informed consent, every time

Consent means the person understands where the story will appear, who might see it, and that the internet is permanent. Ask clearly, in plain language, and get it in writing for anything beyond casual social posts. Consent from a parent or guardian is required for minors, and even then, ask whether the child's identity needs to be shown at all.

2. Minimize identifying details

Do people really need to know the street, the school or the employer? Often, no. Remove details that could let someone be located or targeted, especially in stories about health, money, abuse, immigration status or conflict.

3. Avoid poverty and pity framing

Stories of hardship should not reduce people to their hardship. Show agency: what the person is doing, deciding and building. Use photos that preserve dignity.

Tell the story with someone, not about them.

4. Verify before you amplify

Viral stories sometimes turn out to be false or exaggerated. Before publishing or resharing, confirm key facts with the person and, where possible, an independent source.

5. Be transparent about money

If a story leads to fundraising, explain who controls the funds and how they will be used. If content is sponsored, label it. Trust is the only currency storytelling really has.

6. Respect the right to be forgotten

Circumstances change. If someone asks for their story to be updated, anonymized or removed, take the request seriously and respond quickly.
