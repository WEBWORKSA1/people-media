How to Get Featured in the Media: A Founder's PR Checklist

Editors are not looking for companies. They are looking for people with a story their readers will care about. Here's how to become one.

By People.Media Editorial·20 October 2026·8 min read
PR

Earned media — coverage you do not pay for directly — remains one of the most credible signals a founder, creator or professional can have. It is also one of the most misunderstood. Most pitches fail for predictable reasons: they are about the company instead of a person, they are not timely, or they ask the editor to do all the work.

The angle comes first

Editors decide in seconds. Before you write a word, answer: why would a stranger care about this today? Strong angles usually fall into one of these buckets:

  • Against the odds: a real obstacle overcome, with specifics.
  • Firsts and records: the first in a community, a field, a region.
  • Trend proof: your story illustrates something bigger happening right now.
  • Useful expertise: you can explain something readers are confused about.
  • Human impact: measurable change in other people's lives.

Build a press-ready kit

Have these ready before you pitch so you can respond within the hour when an editor says yes:

  1. A 50-word and a 150-word bio, written in third person.
  2. Three high-resolution photos (portrait, at work, landscape orientation).
  3. Key facts: founding date, location, milestones, numbers you can verify.
  4. Two or three quotable lines that sound like you.
  5. Contact details and availability for a call.

Write a pitch that respects time

Keep the email under 150 words. Subject line: the angle, not your company name. First line: why this matters now. Second paragraph: the proof. Close: what you can offer (interview, data, photos) and a simple question. Personalize the first sentence to the specific editor's recent work — it shows you actually read the publication.

Pitch the story, not the product. The product will come up naturally.

Follow up once, then move on

One polite follow-up after four to five business days is standard. After that, pitch a different outlet or a different angle. Editors remember people who are easy to work with and gracious when the answer is no.

Turn one feature into many

When a feature runs, add it to your website, your email signature, your pitch decks and your social profiles. Mention it in your next pitch: prior coverage makes the next editor's decision easier. Coverage compounds.

Paid features vs earned coverage

Many reputable publishers offer clearly labelled sponsored or partner features alongside independent editorial. Both can be valuable; what matters is transparency.
