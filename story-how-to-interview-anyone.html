How to Interview Anyone: Questions That Unlock Real Stories

Great interviews are not interrogations. They are guided conversations. Here are the questions and habits professionals rely on.

By People.Media Editorial·05 October 2026·7 min read
Interviewing

Whether you are recording a grandparent, profiling a local entrepreneur or hosting a podcast, the quality of the story depends on the quality of the questions. Good interviewers ask fewer, better questions — and then listen longer than feels comfortable.

Prepare, then let go of the script

Research the person beforehand so you do not waste their time on basics. Write ten to fifteen questions, then mark the three you most need answered. During the interview, treat the list as a safety net, not a script. The best moments usually come from follow-up questions you could not have planned.

Questions that open people up

  • "Take me back to the day you decided to…" — invites a scene, not a summary.
  • "What did you believe then that you don't believe now?" — reveals change.
  • "Who was in the room?" — brings in other characters.
  • "What almost stopped you?" — surfaces the obstacle.
  • "What does no one ask you about that you wish they would?" — hands them the floor.
  • "What would you tell the version of yourself from ten years ago?" — draws out meaning.

Follow-ups do the heavy lifting

When someone says something interesting, do not move on. Ask: "What do you mean by that?", "What happened next?", "How did that feel?" or simply "Say more." These four prompts will carry most interviews.

Silence is a question. Count to five before you speak again.

Make it safe

Explain how the conversation will be used, where it will be published and that they can skip any question. Ask permission before recording. For sensitive topics, offer to let them review quotes for accuracy (not for rewriting). Trust produces honesty, and honesty produces stories worth reading.

Record well

Use a quiet room with soft furnishings. Place the phone or recorder about 20–30 cm from the speaker. Do a ten-second test and listen back. Record a backup on a second device if the conversation is irreplaceable.

After the interview

Transcribe while it is fresh. Highlight the moment, the want, the obstacle, the turning point and the change — the same building blocks described in our storytelling framework. Send a thank-you message within 24 hours.
