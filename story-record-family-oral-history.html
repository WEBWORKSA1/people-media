Record Your Family's Oral History This Weekend: A Starter Kit

The stories of the people who raised us disappear quietly. An afternoon and a phone are enough to save them.

By People.Media Editorial·10 August 2026·7 min read
Family

Most families have a moment when someone says, "I wish we had asked Grandma about that." Oral history — recorded conversations about lived experience — is the simplest way to make sure that moment never comes. You need no special equipment, only curiosity and a little preparation.

What you need

  • A smartphone with a voice recorder app (and enough free storage).
  • A quiet room and a comfortable chair for your relative.
  • Water, a box of old photographs and about ninety minutes.

Questions to get started

Early life: Where did you grow up? What did your home look like? What chores did you have? What did you do for fun?

Family: What do you remember about your parents and grandparents? How did you meet your partner? What traditions did our family keep?

Work and world: What was your first job? What big events do you remember living through? What changed the most during your life?

Reflection: What are you proudest of? What would you do differently? What do you want future generations to know?

Tips for a great session

  • Use photographs as prompts — they unlock memories questions can't.
  • Let silence happen. Memories take time to surface.
  • Ask for specifics: "What did it smell like?", "What were you wearing?"
  • Record in shorter sessions if your relative tires easily.

Preserve and share

Name files clearly (name_date_topic), back them up in two places and consider transcribing the most important parts. Share with family members, and ask your relative which stories they're happy for the wider world to hear.

Every family is an archive. Most of it has never been recorded.
