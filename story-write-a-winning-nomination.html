How to Write a Nomination That Wins

Judges read hundreds of nominations. The ones that stand out share four qualities. Here's a template you can adapt for any award.

By People.Media Editorial·20 September 2026·6 min read
Awards

Award panels, fellowship committees and editors reviewing nominations face the same challenge: separating genuine impact from good adjectives. A winning nomination does that work for them. It is specific, evidenced, focused and human.

1. Lead with impact, not biography

Your first two sentences should state what changed because of this person. Numbers help, but only real ones: people served, money raised, hours given, outcomes improved. If a number cannot be verified, describe the change qualitatively instead.

2. Show evidence

Link to coverage, testimonials, photos, reports or social posts that document the work. Evidence turns a claim into a fact. Judges notice when a nomination is easy to verify.

3. Tell one story

After the evidence, include one short anecdote that captures who this person is: a moment when their character showed. One vivid example makes a nominee memorable in a pile of hundreds.

4. Answer "why now?"

Explain why recognition matters this year: a milestone reached, a project at a critical stage, or a moment when visibility could unlock support.

A template

Opening
[Name] has [specific achievement], resulting in [measurable change].
Evidence
Links, figures, testimonials.
Story
One moment that shows their character.
Why now
What recognition would make possible.
Your relationship
How you know them and how long.

Common reasons nominations fail

  • Vague praise without examples.
  • Listing every achievement instead of the most important three.
  • Missing contact details for the nominee.
  • Submitting at the deadline with no time to fix errors.
